Additional Notes on File Relationships, Context, or Content
(for example, if a user wants to reuse and/or cite your data,
what information would you want them to know?):

   - All raw data are produced by the dynamic bi-level multiagent system
     (DBL-MAS) presented in the manuscript; the simulation is run for a
     10-year horizon on a hypothetical case study modelled after the
     Boulder City - Yuma section of the Colorado River Lower Basin.
   - Each body-text figure aggregates data from 3 water allocation schemes
     (WTS = Water Trading Scheme, WQS = Water Quota Scheme, WTaS = Water
     Tariff Scheme) crossed with up to 4 performance metrics.  The annual
     series are stored per year (Year 1 ... Year 10).
   - "LT mean (PV)" is the 10-year present-value mean of the annual series
     (1/(1 + dr) * arithmetic mean, with dr = 0.05, the discount rate used
     in the simulation), reported in $/m^3 or as a Gini / ratio value.
   - "ST baseline" is the equivalent value obtained from the 1-year
     short-term IGWM simulation (used in the manuscript as a comparison
     reference for the long-term outcome).

File Naming Convention:  The file is named by the names of the figures in
the paper.  Each sheet inside the workbook follows the same naming pattern.

DATA DESCRIPTION FOR: [Figure 4 (lt_perf)]
============================================================

Figure 4 plots the long-term performance of the watershed-scale IGWM under
the three water allocation schemes (WTS, WQS, WTaS) on a 4-row x 3-column
panel grid:

   Rows:  Unit Cost ($/m^3) | Gini coefficient | Available surface
          water ratio      | Water cycle ratio
   Cols:  WTS               | WQS              | WTaS

Within each panel the solid line with markers shows the annual long-term
IGWM trajectory over the 10-year simulation, the dashed line marks the
long-term present-value mean, and the dotted line marks the 1-year
short-term baseline.  Numerical long-term (LT) and short-term (ST) values
are inset in each panel of the figure and replicated in the workbook.

1. Number of variables: 4 metrics x 1 scenario (long-term IGWM) x
                         3 schemes = 12 series
   Number of years per series: 10 (Year 1 ... Year 10)
   Plus 12 long-term present-value means and 12 short-term baselines.

2. Number of cases/rows: 3 (one row per scheme, four blocks total) +
                          12 (3 schemes x 4 metrics) LT/ST summary columns

3. Missing data codes: N/A

4. Variable List

   A. Name: <Unit Cost ($/m^3)>
      Description: <This metric measures the cost level of water
      resources allocation within the watershed.  It is calculated by
      dividing the total present-value IGWM costs by the total amount
      of water supplied in the watershed over the 10-year horizon.>
      Value labels if appropriate: USD per cubic metre.

   B. Name: <Gini coefficient>
      Description: <Water allocation Gini coefficient.  This metric
      quantifies the equity level of water resources allocation within
      the watershed.  It is computed from the per-capita water volumes
      across the seven urban areas (see Eq. C404a in the manuscript).>
      Value labels if appropriate: dimensionless, [0, 1].

   C. Name: <Available surface water ratio>

      Value labels if appropriate: dimensionless, [0, 1].

      Description: <This metric measures the degree of urban stored

      Value labels if appropriate: dimensionless, [0, 1].

DATA DESCRIPTION FOR: [Figure 6 (c_g_sens)]
============================================================

Figure 6 shows the sensitivity of long-term IGWM mean unit water cost
and Gini coefficient to two socio-hydrologic drivers across the WTS,
WQS, and WTaS allocation schemes on a 2-row x 2-column panel grid:

   Rows:  Mean unit water cost ($/m^3) | Gini coefficient
   Cols:  Discount rate sweep         | Hydroclimatic forcing sweep
           (0% - 10%, baseline 5%)       (50% - 150% of baseline)

   Panel (a) cost vs. discount rate
   Panel (b) cost vs. hydroclimatic
   Panel (c) Gini vs. discount rate
   Panel (d) Gini vs. hydroclimatic

1. Number of variables:
   - Discount rate sweep: 2 metrics x 3 schemes x 5 levels = 30 cells
   - Hydroclimatic sweep: 2 metrics x 3 schemes x 5 levels = 30 cells
   Total = 60 cells.

2. Number of cases/rows: 6 (one row per scheme x metric combination,
                          two blocks for the two drivers)

3. Missing data codes: N/A

4. Variable List

   A. Name: <Mean unit water cost ($/m^3)>
      Description: <This metric measures the cost level of water
      resources allocation within the watershed.  It is calculated by
      dividing the total present-value IGWM costs by the total amount
      of water supplied in the watershed over the 10-year horizon.>
      Value labels if appropriate: USD per cubic metre.

   B. Name: <Gini coefficient>
      Description: <Water allocation Gini coefficient.  This metric
      quantifies the equity level of water resources allocation within
      the watershed.  It is computed from the per-capita water volumes
      across the seven urban areas (see Eq. C404a in the manuscript).>
      Value labels if appropriate: dimensionless, [0, 1].

5. Driver sweep ranges
   - Discount rate: 0%, 2.5%, 5% (baseline), 7.5%, 10%
   - Hydroclimatic forcing (upstream inflow and rainfall): 50%, 75%,
     100% (baseline), 125%, 150% of baseline.
