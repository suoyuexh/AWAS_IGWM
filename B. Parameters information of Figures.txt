Additional Notes on File Relationships, Context, or Content
(for example, if a user wants to reuse and/or cite this data,
what information would you want them to know?):

   - All raw data are produced by the dynamic bi-level multiagent system
     (DBL-MAS) presented in the manuscript; the simulation is run for a
     10-year planning horizon on a hypothetical case study modelled after
     the Boulder City - Yuma section of the Colorado River Lower Basin
     (seven river-connected urban areas, UA 1 - UA 7).
   - Each policy-GI scenario and sensitivity setting was simulated across
     ten independent optimization runs under identical inputs and parameter
     bounds.  Across the ten replicate runs, outcomes are summarized by the
     sample mean, the sample standard deviation (SD, denominator nine),
     and the observed minimum-maximum range.
   - Each body-text data figure aggregates data from 3 water allocation
     schemes (WTS = Water Trading Scheme, WQS = Water Quota Scheme, WTaS =
     Water Tariff Scheme) and reports two performance metrics: the mean
     unit water cost and the water allocation Gini coefficient.
   - The present-value convention used in the manuscript is
     horizon summary = mean(annual series) / (1 + dr) with the baseline
     annual discount rate dr = 0.05.
   - "Fixed ST comparator" values correspond to the equivalent metrics
     from a 1-year short-term IGWM simulation (planning horizon Y = 1
     without inter-annual GI accumulation or multi-year demand updates),
     used in the manuscript as fixed comparison references for the
     long-term outcomes.

File Naming Convention:  The workbook is named after its role (raw data of
the figures).  Each sheet inside the workbook follows the naming pattern
"Figure <n> (<internal label>)", where <n> is the body-text figure number
of the revised manuscript.

Figure 7 shows the sensitivity of the horizon mean unit water cost and
the Gini coefficient to two socio-hydrologic drivers across the WTS,
WQS, and WTaS allocation schemes on a 2-row x 2-column panel grid:

   Rows:  Mean unit cost ($/m^3) | Gini coefficient
   Cols:  Discount-rate sweep    | Combined hydroclimatic sweep

   Panel (a) cost vs. discount rate
   Panel (b) cost vs. hydroclimatic level
   Panel (c) Gini vs. discount rate
   Panel (d) Gini vs. hydroclimatic level

Within each panel the lines with markers show the ten-run means at each
driver level and the shaded bands show the observed minimum-maximum run
ranges.  Each level contains ten replicate runs; the sweeps are
one-factor-at-a-time experiments around the common baseline experiment.

1. Number of variables:
   - Discount-rate sweep: 2 metrics x 3 schemes x 3 statistics x
     5 levels = 90 cells
   - Combined hydroclimatic sweep: 2 metrics x 3 schemes x 3 statistics
     x 5 levels = 90 cells
   Total = 180 cells.

2. Number of cases/rows: 18 (one row per metric x scheme x statistic,
                          two driver blocks total)

      Description: <Horizon (10-year present-value) mean unit water cost
      at each driver level; same definition as in Figure 5.>
      Value labels if appropriate: USD per cubic metre.

   B. Name: <Gini coefficient>
      Description: <Horizon water allocation Gini coefficient at each
      driver level; same definition as in Figure 5.>
